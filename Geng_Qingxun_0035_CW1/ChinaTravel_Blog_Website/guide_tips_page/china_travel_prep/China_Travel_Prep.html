China Travel Prep

– What You Need to Know Before You Go

Hey travelers! Planning a trip to Mainland China is exciting—but a little prep goes a long way to make your journey smooth and fun. Whether you’re visiting for 5 days or a month, here’s everything you need to pack, how to stay connected, and key cultural tips to help you fit right in.

First: What to Pack (The Essentials!)

You don’t need to overpack, but these items will save you hassle:

• Comfortable shoes:

Mainland China has so much to explore on foot—think walking the Great Wall, wandering ancient towns, or hiking in Zhangjiajie. Bring 1-2 pairs of sneakers or supportive walking shoes (avoid new shoes that might rub!).

• Weather-friendly clothes:

Check the season of your trip! In winter (Dec-Feb), northern China (like Harbin) can be -20°C (-4°F)—pack a thick down jacket, hat, gloves, and thermal underwear. In summer (Jun-Aug), southern China (like Guangzhou) is hot and humid—light, breathable shirts, shorts, and a rain jacket (sudden showers are common!). For spring (Mar-May) or autumn (Sep-Nov), bring layers (a sweater + light jacket) since temperatures can change quickly.

• Power adapter:

Mainland China uses Type A, Type C, and Type I plugs (220V). A universal adapter works for most devices, but a compact one is easier to carry.

• Travel-sized toiletries:

Many hotels provide basic soap and shampoo, but if you have favorite products (or sensitive skin), bring small bottles (under 100ml if flying).

• A small backpack/purse:

For daily outings—carry your phone, wallet, water bottle, and a portable charger (China’s cities are big, and you’ll use your phone a lot!).

• Important documents:

Keep a physical copy and a digital backup (in your phone or email) of your passport, visa, flight/hotel confirmations, and travel insurance. A small pouch around your neck (hidden under clothes) is a safe way to carry passports.

Stay Connected: Wi-Fi & SIM Cards

You won’t want to get lost or miss sharing photos—here’s how to stay online:

• Free Wi-Fi:

Most hotels, restaurants, cafes, and airports in big cities (Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou) have free Wi-Fi. Just ask the staff for the password (it’s usually simple, like the restaurant’s phone number).

• Local SIM card:

For more flexibility (especially when traveling to small towns or rural areas), buy a Chinese SIM card. You can get one at the airport (look for booths like China Mobile, China Unicom, or China Telecom) or online before your trip. You’ll need your passport to register it. A 10-15 day plan with 10GB of data usually costs around $15-$25—enough for maps, messaging, and photos.

• Translation apps offline:

Download Google Translate or Microsoft Translator before you go, and save the Chinese language pack for offline use. This is a lifesaver for ordering food or asking for directions when Wi-Fi is spotty.

Cultural Tips: Be Respectful & Confident

Chinese people are friendly, and small gestures go a long way. Here are easy tips to remember:

• Chopsticks etiquette:

Don’t stick chopsticks straight up in your rice (it’s associated with funerals). Don’t wave chopsticks around, and don’t use them to point at people. If you’re not good with chopsticks, most restaurants will give you a fork or spoon if you ask (just say “kuài zi bù hǎo yòng, qǐng gěi wǒ chā zi” – “I’m not good with chopsticks, please give me a fork”).

• Greetings:

A simple “nǐ hǎo” (hello) or “xièxie” (thank you) will make people smile. You don’t need to bow—just a friendly nod works.

• Queueing:

Chinese people value order—always wait in line for buses, trains, or to buy tickets. Cutting the line is considered rude.

• Shoes indoors:

When visiting someone’s home or some temples, you may be asked to take off your shoes. Just follow the host’s lead—there will usually be slippers to wear.

• Photography:

Ask before taking photos of people (especially elders or in rural areas). Some temples or museums don’t allow photos inside—look for signs that say “bù xǔ pāi zhào” (no photos).

Final Thought

Prepping for Mainland China is simple—focus on comfort, connection, and respect. You’ll probably make small mistakes (everyone does!), but people will appreciate your effort to adapt. Now you’re ready to enjoy all the amazing things Mainland China has to offer—happy packing, and safe travels!
