Top 3 Food in Guilin

Guilin Rice Noodles (Guìlín Mǐmiàn)

Introduction Taste Price
Introduction

Guilin Rice Noodles are Guilin’s signature dish—thin, chewy rice noodles served in a savory broth (or sometimes dry, with sauce) and topped with pickled mustard greens, shredded pork, peanuts, and chili oil. The broth is made by simmering pork bones, star anise, and cinnamon for hours, giving it a deep, aromatic flavor. What makes Guilin’s version unique is the “sour bamboo shoots” (pickled bamboo shoots) that add a tangy kick—locals say you can’t have authentic Guilin noodles without them. It’s a breakfast staple for Guilin locals, but you’ll find it at street stalls and restaurants all day long.

Taste

Noodles: Thin, chewy, and slightly elastic—soak up the broth’s flavor.

Broth: Savory, aromatic, with a hint of star anise; not too salty.

Toppings: Sour bamboo shoots add tang, shredded pork adds savoriness, and chili oil adds a warm heat.

Overall: Hearty but light—perfect for starting your day.

Price

Street Stalls: 8–12 CNY/bowl (basic version with all toppings).

Local Restaurants: 15–20 CNY/bowl (larger portion, with extra pork or beef).

Osmanthus Cake (Guìhuā Gāo)

Introduction Taste Price
Introduction

Osmanthus Cake is a sweet, fragrant dessert that’s a symbol of Guilin—made with glutinous rice flour, sugar, and fresh osmanthus flowers (Guilin is famous for its osmanthus, which blooms in autumn). The cake has a soft, chewy texture and a strong osmanthus aroma—sweet but not cloying. It’s usually steamed in small bamboo baskets, giving it a rustic look, and served warm or cold. You’ll find it at street stalls, dessert shops, and night markets in Guilin—locals often buy it as a snack or a gift for friends.

Taste

Texture: Soft, chewy, and slightly sticky (like mochi but lighter).

Flavor: Sweet, with a strong, fresh osmanthus aroma—no artificial flavors.

Overall: Light and fragrant—perfect for after a spicy meal.

Price

Street Stalls: 5–8 CNY/piece (small bamboo basket).

Dessert Shops: 12–18 CNY/box (4–6 pieces, packaged as a gift).

Spicy Fried River Fish (Liú Yú Chǎo)

Introduction Taste Price
Introduction

Spicy Fried River Fish is a hearty Guilin dish made with small fresh fish from the Li River (usually 10–15cm long, like “small silver fish”). The fish are cleaned, marinated in chili powder and soy sauce, then deep-fried until crispy (you can eat the whole fish—bones and all). It’s often stir-fried with garlic, ginger, and fresh chili peppers for extra heat, and sometimes served with a side of rice or beer. The fish’s small size means they cook quickly, so the meat stays tender inside while the outside is crispy. It’s a popular dish at local restaurants and family gatherings—especially in autumn, when Li River fish are at their freshest.

Taste

Outside: Crispy, slightly salty, with a spicy kick from chili powder.

Inside: Tender, sweet fish meat (no fishy taste, thanks to fresh Li River fish).

Seasoning: Garlic and ginger add aroma, while fresh chili peppers add a bright, fiery heat.

Overall: Crispy, spicy, and savory—great with rice or beer.

Price

Local Restaurants (Guilin Downtown): 48–68 CNY/serving (serves 2–3 people, uses fresh Li River fish).

Yangshuo Riverside Eateries: 58–78 CNY/serving (riverfront views, larger portion of fish).

Night Markets: 15–20 CNY/small plate (for a snack, uses smaller fish).
