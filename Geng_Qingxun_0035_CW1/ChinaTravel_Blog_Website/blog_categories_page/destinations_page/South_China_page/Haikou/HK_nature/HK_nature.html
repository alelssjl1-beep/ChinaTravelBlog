Top 3 Nature Spots in Haikou

Dongzhaigang Mangrove Nature Reserve

Introduction Best Time to Visit Price
Introduction

Dongzhaigang Mangrove Nature Reserve, 40 kilometers east of Haikou, is China’s first national mangrove reserve—covering 5,400 hectares of lush wetlands where mangrove trees grow in brackish water (a mix of sea and river water). The reserve is home to over 20 species of mangroves (e.g., the “red mangrove” with aerial roots that twist into the mud) and 150+ bird species (including egrets, herons, and migratory birds in winter). The best way to explore is by taking a wooden boat tour (1–1.5 hours): you’ll glide through narrow waterways, watch fish jump between mangrove roots, and spot birds nesting in the canopy. The reserve stays cool (20°C–28°C year-round) and is a peaceful escape from Haikou’s city buzz.

Best Time to Visit

Morning (8:30 AM–10:30 AM): Birds are most active; low tide reveals mangrove roots.

Winter (November–February): Migratory birds arrive; weather is cool and dry.

Price

Entry Fee: 25 CNY/adult; 12 CNY/students (with ID); free for kids under 1.2m.

Wooden Boat Tour: 50 CNY/person (private boat for 2–4 people: 150 CNY total).

Haikou Bay

Introduction Best Time to Visit Price
Introduction

Haikou Bay is a 10-kilometer-long coastal stretch in downtown Haikou—where golden sandy beaches meet turquoise sea, and coconut palms sway in the ocean breeze. Unlike Sanya’s crowded beaches, Haikou Bay is quiet and local-friendly: you can walk along the “Haikou Bay Coastal Promenade” (a paved path with benches and ocean views), build sandcastles on the beach, or rent a bike (30 CNY/hour) to cycle along the coast. At sunset, the sky turns pink and orange over the sea—locals gather here to fly kites, have picnics, or just watch the sun go down. There are also small cafes and seafood stalls near the beach, making it easy to grab a snack while enjoying the views.

Best Time to Visit

Afternoon (4:00 PM–6:00 PM): Cooler temperatures; perfect for beach walks and sunset views.

Winter (December–January): Mild (20°C–24°C), no strong winds.

Price

Entry Fee: Free (beach and promenade are open to the public).

Bike Rental: 30 CNY/hour; 80 CNY/day (includes a lock).

Seafood Stalls: 30–50 CNY/person (for grilled shrimp or squid).

Five Finger Mountain (Wuzhi Mountain) – Haikou Section

Introduction Best Time to Visit Price
Introduction

Five Finger Mountain, Hainan’s highest mountain (1,867 meters), has a scenic section just 90 kilometers from Haikou—offering lush tropical forests, gurgling waterfalls, and misty peaks. Unlike the main Five Finger Mountain (near Sanya), Haikou’s section is less crowded and ideal for casual hiking: well-paved trails wind through forests of banyan trees and ferns, leading to small waterfalls (e.g., “Green Valley Waterfall”) where you can dip your feet in cool water. The air here is fresh (filled with the scent of tropical plants), and the temperature is 5°C–8°C cooler than Haikou’s downtown—making it a great summer escape. At the trail’s midpoint, there’s a viewing platform with views of rolling green mountains stretching to the horizon.

Best Time to Visit

Morning (9:00 AM–12:00 PM): Cool for hiking; mist lifts to reveal mountain views.

Spring (March–April): Wildflowers bloom; no heavy rain.

Price

Entry Fee: 40 CNY/adult; 20 CNY/students.

Guided Hike (2 hours): 80 CNY/person (includes a local guide to point out plants and wildlife).
