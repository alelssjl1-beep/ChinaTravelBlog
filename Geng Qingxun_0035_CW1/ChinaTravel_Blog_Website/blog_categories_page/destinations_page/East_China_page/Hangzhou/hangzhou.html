Hangzhou Travel Blog: Fall in Love with China’s "City of Heaven"

Hangzhou, often called the "City of Heaven" (Tianxia Suzhou-Hangzhou), lives up to every bit of its reputation. Nestled in eastern China’s Zhejiang Province, this city is where misty lakes, ancient temples, and lush tea plantations come together in perfect harmony. My 4-day trip here felt like stepping into a Chinese ink painting—every morning, I’d wake up to the view of West Lake’s willow trees swaying over calm water; every afternoon, I’d hike through bamboo forests or sip Longjing tea in a mountain village; and every evening, I’d stroll along illuminated lake shores, listening to locals play traditional music.

What makes Hangzhou special is its balance of natural beauty and cultural depth. It’s not just about West Lake (though that’s reason enough to visit)—it’s about the stories behind every bridge, the history in every temple, and the care in every cup of tea. I ate my way through street food stalls selling crispy fried glutinous rice cakes and steamed beggar’s chicken, wandered through 1,000-year-old temples where monks still chant, and even took a boat ride on West Lake at sunset, watching the sky turn pink over distant hills.

Whether you’re a nature lover, a history buff, or a foodie, Hangzhou has a way of slowing you down and making you savor the moment. Below, I’ll share my favorite spots, essential travel tips, and detailed guides to the three scenic spots, three historic sites, and three must-try foods that made my trip unforgettable.

Travel Tips for Hangzhou

Best Time to Visit:

Tip 1: Spring (March–April): Cherry blossoms and peach blossoms bloom along West Lake’s shores; Longjing tea is harvested (the most famous "pre-Ming" tea is picked this season).

Tip 2: Autumn (September–November): Cool temperatures (15°C–25°C), clear skies, and red maple leaves in Lingyin Temple’s bamboo forests—ideal for hiking and lake activities.

Tip 3: Avoid Summer (June–August): Hot (28°C–35°C) and humid, with frequent rain; winter (December–February) is cool (5°C–12°C) but quiet, great for temple visits.

Transportation:

Tip 1: Getting to Hangzhou:

Fly to Hangzhou Xiaoshan International Airport (40 minutes to city center by subway Line 1, 7 CNY; or taxi ~120 CNY). High-speed trains from Shanghai (1 hour, ~70 CNY) or Nanjing (2 hours, ~150 CNY) are also convenient.

Tip 2: Getting Around:

Metro: Subway (single ride 2–6 CNY)

Bikes: shared bikes (2 CNY/30 minutes)—perfect for cycling around West Lake (bike lanes are well-marked).

Taxis/Didi: taxis (start at 13 CNY)

Essential Tips:

Tip 1: Get a Public Transport Card: Hangzhou’s subway (Lines 1–10) and buses cover most attractions—buy a reusable "Hangzhou Tourism Card" (20 CNY deposit) to save time on ticket purchases.

Tip 2: Book West Lake Boat Tickets in Advance: Especially on weekends, boat rentals (rowboats, electric boats) sell out fast—book online via the "West Lake Scenic Area" official WeChat account.

Tip 3: Respect Tea Culture: When visiting Longjing Village, ask before taking photos of tea farmers at work; when drinking Longjing tea, sip slowly (it’s meant to be tasted, not gulped).
